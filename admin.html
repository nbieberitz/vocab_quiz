How to write the list

One word per line: word | definition | example sentence. The example is optional. (Copying three columns from a spreadsheet also works.)

A line starting with # starts a new lesson and gives its name. Words before the first # line go in lesson 1.

A line starting with ~ lists words that are easy to mix up, separated by commas (2 to 8 words). The quiz uses them as wrong options and shows a warning.

# Language
annotation | A critical or explanatory note | She left an annotation on nearly every page.
parlance | A particular manner of speaking | In the parlance of the trading floor, a crash is "going off a cliff."
patois | A regional dialect | The villagers spoke a patois visitors could barely follow.

# Possibility
contingent | Conditional; dependent on other things | His place was contingent on a background check.
implausible | Difficult to believe | The alibi was implausible.

~ parlance, patois

Words can use letters, digits, spaces, hyphens and apostrophes. Every word and every definition must be different. A quiz needs at least 4 words.
